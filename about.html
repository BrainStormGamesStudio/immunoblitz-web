About BrainStorm Games

Independent educational game development from Malta, combining science, strategy and digital learning.

BrainStorm Games is an independent educational game studio based in Malta. Our focus is creating compact digital experiences that make complex ideas easier to explore through interaction, strategy and play. Rather than treating games as a replacement for teaching, we use them as a bridge between curiosity and deeper learning.

Our flagship project, ImmunoBlitz, turns the invisible world of infection and immunity into a strategy challenge. Players face pathogens, protect the body, manage limited resources and make decisions involving natural defences, medical interventions and public-health tools. The goal is to give learners a memorable context for asking scientific questions.

Who Created ImmunoBlitz?

ImmunoBlitz was created by Jacob Azzopardi, a Maltese science educator with a background in biology education and digital learning. The project grew alongside postgraduate research in Digital Education at the University of Edinburgh exploring how a science videogame could influence motivation, flow and enjoyment among middle-school science students.

This combination of classroom experience, science education and educational-technology research shapes the way ImmunoBlitz is designed: the game should be engaging, but it should also give teachers and learners something meaningful to discuss after play ends.

Why the Immune System?

Immunology is full of processes that students cannot see directly. Pathogens are microscopic, immune cells communicate through chemical signals, and many protective mechanisms happen automatically inside tissues. These ideas can feel abstract when they are encountered only through diagrams or definitions.

ImmunoBlitz makes selected relationships visible. Threats appear, body systems come under pressure, defensive choices have consequences and different interventions serve different purposes. This does not turn the game into a literal simulation of human physiology. Instead, it creates an interactive model that learners can question and compare with real science.

What Players Explore

Pathogens

Viruses, bacteria and fungi are treated as distinct biological threats rather than one generic category of “germs”.

First-Line Defences

Skin, mucus and other barriers introduce the idea that immunity begins before pathogens reach internal tissues.

Cellular Immunity

The game and Learning Hub connect players with macrophages, neutrophils, lymphocytes, antibodies and immune memory.

Prevention

Vaccination, hygiene and other public-health ideas encourage players to think beyond treatment after infection has already developed.

Medical Interventions

Antibiotics and other tools are presented as context-dependent responses, helping reinforce that not every treatment works against every pathogen.

Scientific Models

Learners are encouraged to ask what each game mechanic represents and where the game deliberately simplifies reality.

Our Educational Approach

BrainStorm Games follows a simple principle: play should create questions. A successful educational game should not merely display facts on top of gameplay. It should encourage the player to make a decision, observe a consequence and want to understand why it happened.

For this reason, ImmunoBlitz is supported by a growing Learning Hub. The game introduces concepts quickly, while the articles provide deeper explanations, authoritative sources and explicit comparisons between the game model and real biology.

What we want learners to do

  • recognise that different pathogens require different responses;
  • understand that the body uses several overlapping layers of defence;
  • distinguish prevention from treatment;
  • connect immune memory with vaccination;
  • understand why antibiotics target bacteria rather than viruses;
  • question and evaluate scientific models instead of accepting them literally.

Designed for Learning, Not Just Winning

ImmunoBlitz uses familiar game ideas such as waves, upgrades, health indicators and resource management because they make the experience readable and strategic. These mechanics are intentionally simplified. Real infections do not occur in numbered rounds and immune cells do not appear because a player spends points.

That difference is part of the learning opportunity. The website repeatedly identifies where game rules diverge from biology so that students can compare the model with scientific explanations rather than developing misconceptions from the game alone.

For Students, Teachers and Families

The game is designed primarily for school-age learners studying microorganisms, infection, immunity and public health, but it can also be used by teachers, parents and curious adults. Teachers can use the game as a lesson starter, discussion prompt or revision activity and then direct students to the Learning Hub for deeper reading.

For structured classroom use, see our Teacher's Guide to Using ImmunoBlitz, which includes observation tasks, discussion questions, model-evaluation activities and assessment ideas.

About the Research Behind the Project

ImmunoBlitz also developed as part of academic work examining game-based science learning. The associated postgraduate research focused on student motivation, flow and enjoyment when a science videogame was used with middle-school learners. That research background continues to influence the project's emphasis on engagement without losing sight of educational purpose.

Looking Ahead

BrainStorm Games aims to keep improving ImmunoBlitz through clearer scientific explanations, accessibility improvements, technical updates and feedback from players and educators. New Learning Hub resources will continue to expand the educational value of the website beyond the game itself.

We also welcome thoughtful feedback from teachers and learners. If you have used ImmunoBlitz in a classroom, encountered a technical problem or have an idea for a future learning resource, visit the Contact page.

A note about scientific accuracy

ImmunoBlitz is an educational game, not a medical simulator. Human immunology is substantially more complex than the systems represented in gameplay. The Learning Hub and Educational & Medical Disclaimer explain these limitations in more detail.
